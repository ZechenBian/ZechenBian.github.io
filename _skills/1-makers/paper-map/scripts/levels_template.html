How this page works

Each paper gets a level — how much of it this purpose needs:

  • Full: all of it.
  • Partial: the results this purpose uses, plus everything they rest on inside the paper (definitions and proofs), plus the first two pages.
  • Statements: only what you need to read those results — the results and the definitions their statements use, plus the first two pages; no proofs.
  • Reference: nothing to read; the paper is only cited.

Inside each paper the results fall into four layers, computed from the dependency map: core (used by the purpose — linked from the story, cited across papers, or named by the open problem), needed for the statements (definitions the core statements use, transitively), needed for the proofs (everything else the core rests on), and not needed. Pin ★ any result to make it core; unpin a core result to drop it. The page strip shows every PDF page: dark = core, medium = statements, light = proofs; outlined = in your reading set.

Suggested levels for the story are Claude's reading of what the story uses, with a reason under each paper; for an open problem, papers it names are suggested as Partial and the rest as Reference. Your choices are saved with this page (when saving is available) so that Claude can read them back when you ask for a new summary or add a paper.
