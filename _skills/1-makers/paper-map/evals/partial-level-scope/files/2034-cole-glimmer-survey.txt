A survey of glimmers
J. Cole
2034

1. Basics
1.1 Definition. A glimmer is a pair (F, s) as in [A2, Definition 1.1].
1.4 Lemma. The zero scheme of a glimmer is reduced.
Proof. A double zero would count twice in deg F. ∎

2. History
2.1 Proposition. Ash's theorem [A2, Theorem 1.3] fails for sheaves that are not glimmers.
Proof. Take F = O ⊕ O(2) with a section vanishing twice at one point. ∎

3. Glimmers and twists
3.1 Definition. The twist number t(F, s) is the least n with F(n) a sum of line bundles.
3.2 Theorem. For a glimmer on P^1, t(F, s) ≤ 1, with equality unless F is already split.
Proof. The upper bound is [A2, Theorem 1.3]; for equality use Lemma 1.4 and Definition 3.1. ∎

4. Open questions
4.1 Question. Is t bounded on surfaces?
4.2 Proposition. On an elliptic curve, t(F, s) ≤ 2.
Proof. Combine [B, Theorem 2.1] with Proposition 2.1. ∎
