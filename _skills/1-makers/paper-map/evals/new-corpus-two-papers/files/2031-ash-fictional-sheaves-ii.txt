Fictional sheaves II
R. Ash
2031

Abstract. We study glimmers, a fictional kind of sheaf on a curve, and prove that
every glimmer of degree d splits after one twist (Theorem 1.3).

1. Glimmers
1.1 Definition. A glimmer on a curve X is a pair (F, s) where F is a sheaf on X and
s is a section of F that vanishes at exactly deg(F) points.
1.2 Lemma. If (F, s) is a glimmer, then F(1) has a section s' with s'|U = s on the
complement U of the zeros of s.
Proof. Twist s by the canonical section of O(1). ∎
<<<page 2>>>
1.3 Theorem. Every glimmer (F, s) of degree d ≥ 1 splits after one twist: F(1) is a
sum of line bundles.
Proof. By Lemma 1.2 the twisted section s' exists; by Definition 1.1 its zeros are the
zeros of s, so F(1) is generated off a finite set, and the splitting follows from
Grothendieck's theorem on P^1 [G]. ∎
2. Examples
2.1 Example. For X = P^1 and F = O(2) with s = x^2, Theorem 1.3 gives F(1) = O(3).
<<<page 3>>>
3. Future directions
We expect that Theorem 1.3 holds for glimmers on surfaces, probably after two twists;
the smallest open case is X = P^2 with deg F = 2. We do not know whether the twist is
necessary when d = 1.
